# Break It, Fix It

**Research Methods & Data Analysis I · 30 minutes · follow-up to the Construct Lab**

**Link:** `https://hfgolino.github.io/BuildYourOwnInstrument/break-it/`

## What you will do

You will break a 12-item scale on purpose, six times, and fix it each time. Before you see the data, you predict which numbers will change. The point is to learn which number answers which question, and which numbers can look fine while the scale is broken.

## Steps

1. **Sign in** with your full name and UVA computing ID, the same ID as in the Construct Lab. To use last week's scale, click **Choose my Construct Lab ticket** and pick your Construct Lab exit ticket (the PDF or .txt; you can download it again from your Canvas submission). Without it, choose a standard scale; the lab works the same way.
2. **Baseline (4 min).** Read the evidence board, look at your scale's EGA network (hover an item to see its wording and links), and answer two questions. Press **i** on any number or picture to see its definition.
3. **Round 1: break reliability (9 min).** Pad it, make it a mood, cut it to True / False.
4. **Round 2: break validity (9 min).** Mix in the other trait, use it for hiring, drop a facet.

   For each of the six breaks:
   - **Predict:** for each number shown, will it go up, stay about the same, or go down? A change of .02 or less counts as about the same.
   - **Run it**, read why each number moved, and compare the scale before and after in the pictures (network, semantic map, correlations, or the plot behind the number).
   - **Fix it:** pick one of three changes and run it. If it does not work, try another.
5. **Exit ticket (4 min).** Write the reflection (at least 30 words), click **Create my exit ticket**, then **Save as PDF** and upload it to the Canvas assignment **Break It, Fix It: Exit Ticket**. If the PDF does not work, use **Download as .txt instead**.

There are no points. Canvas marks your ticket complete or incomplete, and your feedback (what you predicted correctly and what to review) appears as a comment.

## The evidence board

> [!NOTE]
> **Cronbach's α.** How much the items agree with each other on one occasion. It rises with more items and with items that repeat each other, so a high α does not show that the items measure the right thing.

> [!NOTE]
> **Test-retest r.** The correlation between the same people's scores two weeks apart. It repeats over time, not over items.

> [!NOTE]
> **SEM.** The typical distance between an observed score and the true score, SEM = SD × √(1 − α), shown as a percentage of the possible score range. Smaller is more precise.

> [!NOTE]
> **Content coverage.** Whether the items still cover all three facets of the trait, and nothing outside it.

> [!NOTE]
> **Structure (EGA).** Whether the items group by facet in a network of partial correlations: how many items sit with their own facet.

> [!NOTE]
> **Convergent r.** Correlation with another measure of the same trait: a friend's rating.

> [!NOTE]
> **Discriminant r.** Correlation with a measure of a different trait. It should be clearly lower than the convergent r (Rust et al. call it divergent validity).

> [!NOTE]
> **Criterion r.** Correlation with an outcome the trait should relate to. Conscientiousness: percent of assignments turned in on time. Openness: arts and culture events attended.

> [!NOTE]
> **Known groups d.** The difference between two groups that should differ on the trait, in standard deviation units. Conscientiousness: students who keep a written planner vs. those who do not. Openness: students who have taken an art or music course vs. those who have not.

> [!TIP]
> Two questions to ask every time: **Repeated over what?** (items or time) and **Evidence for what?** (consistency or meaning).
