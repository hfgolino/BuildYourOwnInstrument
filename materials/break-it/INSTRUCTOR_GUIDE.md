# Break It, Fix It: instructor guide

**Follow-up to the Construct Lab · 30 minutes · link: `https://hfgolino.github.io/BuildYourOwnInstrument/break-it/`**

## Goal

To make two distinctions stick by having students predict them and then see them:

1. **Repeated over what?** Cronbach's α repeats over items on one occasion; test-retest repeats over time. A scale can pass one and fail the other.
2. **Reliable is not valid.** Several breaks leave α unchanged or *raise* it while the meaning of the score gets worse. Each kind of validity evidence (content, structure, convergent, discriminant, criterion, known groups) answers a different question.

## How it works

Each student starts from a 12-item scale: their own Construct Lab scale or a standard Conscientiousness or Openness scale. The Construct Lab scale comes from one of two places:

- **The Construct Lab exit ticket** (the PDF or .txt the student uploaded to Canvas; they can download it again from their submission). The start screen has a *Choose my Construct Lab ticket* button. The ticket is read in the browser, its verification code is checked, and it must carry the same computing ID. This is the reliable route: tell students to have the ticket at hand.
- **The browser's saved progress**, only if the student finished the Construct Lab (12 items locked) on the same browser, under the same computing ID, in a normal window, and the site data were not cleared. Safari deletes a site's saved data after seven days without a visit. When nothing is found, the start screen says why (no saved progress, progress under a different ID, or a lab that was never finished). The scale is given to 300 simulated students twice, two weeks apart. Their friends rate them, and an outcome and a group membership are known for each student. The evidence board shows nine numbers:

| Reliability | Validity |
|---|---|
| Cronbach's α (total and per facet) | Content coverage (facets with 2+ items; off-trait or mood items) |
| Test-retest r (two weeks) | Structure (EGA: items sitting with their own facet) |
| SEM (as % of the score range) | Convergent r (friend's rating of the same trait) |
| | Discriminant r (a separate 12-item measure of the other trait) |
| | Criterion r (C: % of assignments on time; O: arts and culture events attended) |
| | Known groups d (C: keeps a written planner; O: has taken an art or music course) |

Below the board, students see their scale three ways (an interactive EGA network, the semantic map of the 539-item bank, and the item correlation matrix) and the picture behind each number: the test-retest scatter plot, the score distribution with the SEM band, the friend-rating, other-trait, and outcome scatter plots, and the known-groups strips.

Then two rounds of three breaks, in the same order for everyone. For each break the student (1) reads what changed, (2) predicts whether 3 or 4 numbers go up, stay about the same, or go down, (3) runs it, sees every number move on a before-and-after chart, reads why, and compares the two scales side by side in the pictures that matter for that break, and (4) picks a fix (shown against the broken scale in the same pictures) from three options and runs it. A fix that does not work can be followed by another. Every number has an **i** button and there is a Definitions panel on every screen.

## The six breaks (standard Conscientiousness scale; other scales move the same way)

| Break | What changes | Predicted numbers: before → after | Fixes (✓ fixed, ½ partly, ✗ not) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pad it** | 4 reworded near-copies added | α .91 → .94 ↑ · retest .91 → .91 → · convergent .56 → .56 → · structure 12/12 → 14/16 ↓ | ✓ remove the copies · ✗ keep them (higher α) · ✗ add more copies |
| **Make it a mood** | 4 items reworded as "Today I feel…" | α .91 → .88 (small) · retest .91 → .78 ↓ · convergent .56 → .48 ↓ · criterion .53 → .46 ↓ | ✓ trait wording back · ½ retest after one day (retest looks fine, meaning still broken) · ✗ more mood items |
| **Two options** | True / False instead of 5 options | α .91 → .86 ↓ · retest .91 → .85 ↓ · SEM 5.2% → 10.3% ↑ | ✓ back to the original options · ½ keep True / False and add 6 items (Spearman–Brown, at a cost) · ✗ retest after one day |
| **Mix in the other trait** | 4 Openness items added | α .91 → .89 (small) · retest .91 → .91 → · discriminant .26 → .51 ↑ · structure 12/12 → 12/16 ↓ | ✓ remove them · ✗ keep them (α > .70) · ✗ rename it "Conscientiousness and Openness" |
| **Use it for hiring** | applicants self-promote; top 30% hired; outcome only for those hired | α .91 → .93 (same or up) · convergent .56 → .41 ↓ · criterion .53 → .01 ↓ | ✓ anonymous research use, outcome for everyone · ✗ hire only the top 15% · ✗ add 4 items |
| **Drop a facet** | the 4 perseverance items removed (Openness: aesthetics) | content 3 → 2 ↓ · α .91 → .89 (small) · structure 12/12 → 8/8 (clean) · criterion .53 → .46 ↓ | ✓ bring the facet back · ½ rename after the two facets left · ✗ add 4 items to the remaining facets |

**Scoring the predictions.** Each prediction is judged against the student's own data, on the rounded numbers they see: a change of .02 or less counts as "about the same" (SEM: half a percentage point; structure: one item), a change of .04 or more counts as a direction, and in between either answer is accepted. Across 102 simulated student scales, the intended lesson was accepted in 95–100% of cases for the key numbers (for example, padding raises α in 99%, mood wording lowers test-retest in 100%, mixing raises the discriminant r in 100%, hiring lowers the criterion r in 100%). The softer items are α in the mood and drop breaks (it can stay flat or fall a little) and α in the hiring break (it stays flat or rises); the on-screen explanations are written to be true either way, and students are scored against their own data.

## 30-minute plan

| Clock | Stage | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Sign in, load the scale | Students load their Construct Lab ticket (PDF or .txt). Anyone without it uses a standard scale. |
| 2–6 | Baseline board, network, two questions | Ask students to hover one item in the network and open one **i** button. The plot gallery is for browsing, not required. |
| 6–15 | Round 1: pad, mood, two options | Ask the room after "Pad it": who predicted α would go up? Why is that bad news here? |
| 15–24 | Round 2: mix, hiring, drop | After "Use it for hiring": α went up and the criterion collapsed. Which number would a test publisher print? |
| 24–30 | Exit ticket | Upload before leaving. |

Students who finish early can switch back to a round in the top bar and try the fixes they did not choose.

## The pictures for each break

| Break | Opens on | What to point at |
|---|---|---|
| Pad it | EGA network, correlations | Each copy (3′) sits on a thick edge with its original; in the heat map the copy cells are near .95. |
| Make it a mood | Test-retest plot | The cloud widens two weeks later; the mood items (gold dotted rings) still sit with their facets in the network. |
| Two options | Score precision | The score range shrinks to 12–24 and piles up at the top; the SEM band covers a larger share of it. |
| Mix in the other trait | Semantic map | The four added items (squares) land in the other trait's regions of the item bank. |
| Use it for hiring | Outcome | Outcomes exist only to the right of the cutoff; the line is flat there. |
| Drop a facet | Semantic map | Dashed circles mark the removed items: one region of the trait is empty, yet EGA finds a clean structure. |

The semantic map is one UMAP of all 539 bank items (text-embedding-3-small, both traits together). The mood items were written for this lab and were never embedded, so they are listed under the map instead of placed on it.

## Debrief (5 minutes, or the next class)

1. Which breaks left α unchanged or higher while the scale got worse? (Pad, mix, hiring, drop.) What does that say about reporting only α?
2. The mood items had a decent α and a poor test-retest. Which coefficient matches the claim "this scale measures a trait"? (Week 8: repeated over what?)
3. Retesting after one day "fixed" the mood scale's test-retest. Why is that not a real fix? (The interval has to match the claim.)
4. Dropping a facet gave a perfect EGA structure. What did the structure miss? (Content: structure only checks the items you kept.)
5. In the hiring break, why did the criterion correlation fall so far? Two reasons: self-promotion and range restriction.

## What is simulated

The semantic map (`tools/build_breakit_map.py`) uses the real embeddings of the bank items. The responses do not: a new sample of 300 students (seed 20261105) from the same population model as the Construct Lab (`tools/build_breakit_data.py`): two-week retest (trait stability .93 at the factor level), one-day retest (.99, with remembered answers), friend ratings (r ≈ .62 with the trait), outcomes that depend mostly on one facet (perseverance; aesthetics), known groups from the trait, and an applicant condition in which each person self-promotes by a different amount. The 12 mood items ("Today I feel…") were written for this lab, two per facet, and are labeled in the data file. Tell students the respondents are simulated.

## Grading: complete / incomplete, no points

Create the assignment **Break It, Fix It: Exit Ticket** with *Display grade as: Complete/Incomplete* and *File upload* (pdf, txt). The ticket is complete when both baseline questions are answered, all six breaks are finished, and the reflection has at least 30 words. Then:

```bash
pip install pypdf
export CANVAS_URL=https://canvas.its.virginia.edu CANVAS_TOKEN=...
python3 tools/grade_breakit_tickets.py --course COURSE_ID --assignment ASSIGNMENT_ID            # dry run, CSV
python3 tools/grade_breakit_tickets.py --course COURSE_ID --assignment ASSIGNMENT_ID --post     # post
```

Each student gets *complete* or *incomplete* and a comment with their feedback (predictions right, first fixes that worked, and the ideas to review). Edited tickets, tickets uploaded from another account, and identical reflections are flagged and not posted. Without a token: `--dir` on the unzipped *Download Submissions* folder writes the same CSV.
